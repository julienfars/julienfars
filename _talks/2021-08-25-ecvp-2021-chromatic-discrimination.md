Poster presented at the 43rd European Conference on Visual Perception (ECVP 2021), held online, Wednesday 25th August 2021 (Poster Session 5).

**Authors:** Julien Fars, Thiago M.P. Fernandes, Cord Huchzermeyer, Jan Kremers, Galina V. Paramei

We used the Trivector test (Cambridge Colour Test) to assess chromatic discrimination in normal trichromats stratified by age ('young', 'middle-aged', 'mature'). Prompted by the observation that a longer Trivector response window (RW) improves older observers' estimates, we explored whether RW duration affects chromatic discrimination along the Protan, Deutan and Tritan axes. For the 'mature' group, thresholds were significantly higher at a 3-second RW than at an 8-second RW, and Tritan thresholds at 3 seconds were far beyond the expected tolerance limit for this age group — suggestive of spurious, rather than veridical, chromatic discrimination. We suggest that studies testing mature observers with the Trivector test should use a response window of at least 8 seconds to obtain veridical measures of chromatic discrimination.

The full abstract was published in the ECVP 2021 abstract book: [Perception, 50(1S) — 43rd ECVP 2021 Online, Abstracts (PDF)]({{ base_path }}/files/talks/2022-43rd-european-conference-on-visual-perception-(ecvp)-2021-online.pdf).
